ONGOING PROJECT

Dark Pattern

Measure how cognitive load affects the detection of AI dark patterns and understand when and why users fail to notice manipulative AI behavior.

PROJECT

Abstract

AI-driven dark patterns manipulate user decisions, but the psychological conditions enabling this deception remain understudied. This study evaluates how cognitive load impacts user detection of manipulative AI behaviors. In a randomized controlled experiment ($N = 280$), participants interacted with deceptive AI interfaces under low, moderate, and high cognitive loads. High cognitive load reduced dark pattern detection by 42% and significantly increased compliance. Behavioral and eye-tracking data reveal that cognitive depletion forces reliance on System 1 heuristic processing, rendering users blind to manipulative framing and deceptive defaults. These findings prove that vulnerability to AI dark patterns is situational, underscoring the need for cognitive-aware interface safeguards and policy interventions.
