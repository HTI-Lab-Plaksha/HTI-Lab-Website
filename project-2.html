ONGOING PROJECT

ATLAS

Adaptive Technology for Location and Spatial Support: An intelligent, low-cost wearable navigation system designed to provide real-time obstacle awareness and directional guidance to visually impaired individuals.

PROJECT

Abstract

ATLAS (Adaptive Technology for Location and Spatial Support) is an assistive wearable navigation system designed to improve independent mobility for visually impaired individuals. The system combines computer vision and depth sensing to provide real-time environmental awareness without depending on pre-built maps, cloud services, or expensive depth-sensing hardware. Using two VL53L5CX multizone Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensors, ATLAS generates an 8×16 depth matrix with a 90-degree field of view to identify structural hazards such as stairs, drop-offs, and walls. A USB camera connected to a Raspberry Pi 5 runs YOLOv8 for real-time semantic obstacle detection, identifying objects such as people, cars, and chairs. A spatial fusion engine combines visual object detection with depth information to estimate both obstacle identity and distance. The system delivers contextual audio alerts and directional guidance, functioning as a digital "guide dog" to support safer and more independent navigation.

RESEARCH MOTIVATION

The Problem

How can we build an intelligent, low-cost wearable navigation system that delivers real-time obstacle awareness and directional guidance to visually impaired individuals without pre-built maps, cloud dependency, or expensive depth hardware? Existing navigation approaches can face limitations in providing detailed real-time spatial awareness of nearby obstacles and environmental hazards. ATLAS aims to address this challenge through multimodal sensing, computer vision, and real-time spatial understanding.
