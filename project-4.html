RESEARCH PROJECT

A Shared but Individually Scaled EEG Signature of Meditation

Why EEG-based meditation monitors struggle with new people, and what it takes to fix it.

THE PROBLEM

Background & Problem Statement

Electroencephalography (EEG) records the brain's tiny electrical signals through sensors on the scalp, forming rhythms at different speeds (delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma) that shift with mental states. Meditation is a private mental state; researchers rely on instructions or self-report to know when it happens. EEG could track it objectively, but classifiers that separate meditation from mind wandering work well on the person they were trained on and only modestly on anyone new. A practical monitor cannot be retrained for every user. So we ask why transfer fails, and test two explanations.

RESEARCH EXPLANATIONS

Two Possible Explanations

EXPLANATION 01

Individual Signature

Everyone's brain changes in its own way. Nothing transfers between people.

EXPLANATION 02

Shared Signature, Individual Scale

Brains change in a common direction, but by different amounts.

RESEARCH QUESTION

Our Core Question

Is meditation's EEG signal specific to each individual, or shared but too faint to read reliably across people?

METHODOLOGY

Our Approach

We process raw EEG scalp recordings from two open datasets through one shared clean-up (same filters, re-referencing, and eye/muscle artifact removal for everyone) to extract brain-rhythm features. These features represent the share of power in each frequency band, per electrode.

DATA SOURCES

EEG Datasets

DATASET A

Block Design

Practitioners of three traditions (Himalayan Yoga, Vipassana, Isha Shoonya) and non-meditators alternate between meditating and deliberately letting thoughts wander.

DATASET B

Experience Sampling

Meditators are interrupted at random moments and report whether they were meditating or mind wandering.

INVESTIGATION

Key Questions Evaluated

QUESTION 01

Direction

Does the same rhythm change the same way in most people?

QUESTION 02

Transfer

Does a model trained on others recognise meditation in a new person?

QUESTION 03

Personalise

How much of a new person's own data does it take to adapt?

RESULTS

What We're Finding

FINDING 01

A Shared Direction

Theta rhythm power rises during meditation in most people, across traditions and both datasets, and in non-meditators focusing on the breathing techniques. It reflects the meditative state, not years of practice.

FINDING 02

A Genuine Brain Rhythm

The increase survives separating true oscillations from the spectrum's background slope.

FINDING 03

An Individual Size

The shift is small and varies widely between people. A one-size-fits-all model works above chance but modestly, levels off as more people are added, and succeeds mostly for those with a strong shift.

FINDING 04

Calibration Helps, at a Price

A person's own data improves the model, but much of the early gain is learning the person; meditation-specific gains need minutes, not seconds. We combine a population model learned from everyone else with a few minutes of the new person's EEG to create a personalised model.

CONCLUSION

Takeaway

Meditation leaves a shared EEG signature whose strength is specific to the individual subject. Practical monitoring should pair a common model with short personal calibration.
