ABOUT THE LAB

Human-Technology Interaction

The Human-Technology Interaction (HTI) Lab at Plaksha University studies how people and technology interact, with a focus on enhancing human cognitive well-being through a multi-modal sensory approach.

We combine bio-sensors, computer vision, and electro-mechanical sensing to detect and model changes in physiological and behavioral responses including neural activity, facial expressions, heart-rate variability, pupillometry, and galvanic skin response alongside human ergonomics and cognitive state.

Our work is inherently interdisciplinary, drawing on human factors, data science, and public policy to understand the complex interplay between technology and human cognition.

This lets us move from raw physiological signal to actionable insight about how people actually experience the systems they use.

RESEARCH

Research Areas

01

Biosensing

Using sensors to measure physiological signals such as heart rate, heart rate variability (HRV), skin conductance (GSR), and eye movements to understand human health, emotions, and behaviour.

02

Human Factors and Human–AI Interaction

Studying how humans interact with AI systems and robots, focusing on trust, usability, decision-making, safety, and improving collaboration between humans and intelligent systems.

03

Computational Neuroscience and Cognitive Science

Understanding how the human brain works and how people think, learn, remember, and make decisions using computational models and scientific methods.

04

Computer Vision

Developing systems that enable computers to understand and interpret visual information, such as images and videos, for tasks like object detection, facial expression recognition, and human activity analysis.

05

Affective Computing

Developing AI systems that recognize and understand human emotions through facial expressions, voice, and physiological signals.

06

Deep Learning

Using advanced neural network models to learn patterns from large datasets and solve complex problems in areas such as image recognition, speech processing, and predictive analysis.

MULTI-MODAL SENSING

Measuring how humans experience technology.

Our research combines multiple sensing modalities to understand physiological, behavioral, cognitive, and ergonomic responses during human-technology interaction.

Neural Activity

Measuring changes in neural activity during interaction.

Facial Expressions

Understanding behavioral and emotional responses through computer vision.

Heart-Rate Variability

Studying physiological responses associated with cognitive and emotional states.

Pupillometry

Using pupil responses as indicators of cognitive and physiological changes.

Galvanic Skin Response

Measuring changes in electrodermal activity.

Human Ergonomics

Understanding physical interaction, posture, and human-system ergonomics.
